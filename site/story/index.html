Can an AI beat sixty-forty?

We built one allocation engine, plugged in four ways of forecasting the market, and gave every one of them the same data, the same rules and the same scorecard. This is what each approach does, how we kept the contest fair, and what happened.

Scroll to begin ↓
01

The question is older than AI.

Hold 60% stocks and 40% bonds, rebalance once a month, and stop thinking. Investors have used it for decades because it is very hard to beat after costs.

Two funds, one dial

Every approach in this project turns one dial: how much of the money sits in SPY (the 500 largest US companies) and how much in TLT (US government bonds that mature in 20+ years).

The dial starts at 60/40 and may move between 40% and 80% stocks.

Doing nothing is the benchmark

The static 60/40 portfolio is rebalanced to 60/40 each month and pays a small cost when it trades. It never forecasts anything.

An approach earns its place only if it beats this, after its own costs.

Four years of doing nothing

Over the 46 test months, March 2019 to December 2022, 60/40 turned 100 into about 130, through a pandemic crash and the 2022 bond rout.

net CER = 12 × (mean monthly return above cash − 1.25 × its variance)

Its score, a yearly return penalised for bumpiness, is 4.37%. That is the number to beat.

02

One machine, built in nine layers.

Each month-end the same machine runs from data to a score. Only one layer differs between approaches: where the forecast comes from. Follow March 2019 through it.

March 2019 · the deterministic model
03

A model that has read history can cheat.

GPT-4o was trained on years of market news. Show it "March 2019" and it may simply remember what happened next. So the AI never sees the month.

What the AI is shown

A short block of relative numbers: returns over 1, 3 and 12 months, volatility, correlation, where rates and the policy- and geopolitical-risk indexes sit against their last ten years, and how far long yields moved.

What is taken away

No dates. No index names beyond "SPY" and "TLT". No price levels. No headlines. Each one could identify the month.

Checking the mask holds

A separate probe asks the model to guess the year from each masked block. Chance is about 13% within a year. It did no better, except around the 2008 crisis, whose numbers are too extreme to hide.

A mirror test flips stocks and bonds; a model that only reads the numbers should flip its view too.

    04
    Approach one: a classic quant model.
    No AI. Eight economic releases, a regression, and a rule for when to ignore it. It is the transparent baseline every other approach must beat.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Eight surprises
    For each release (jobs, retail sales, industrial production, ISM, CPI, core CPI, PPI, hourly earnings) take what was reported minus what economists expected, from Bloomberg, and scale it by how big surprises usually are.
    z = (actual − consensus) ÷ std of the last 12 surprises
    March 2019 had a big jobs miss (−2.45) and a soft core CPI (−1.66).
    
      
    A regression that can say "no"
    Ridge regression learns how past surprises lined up with the next month's returns. Its one setting, α, decides how far to trust what it learned.
    Q = average + b₁z₁ + … + b₈z₈  ·  minimise Σ(y − Q)² + α Σb²
    Each month it replays past months to pick α. One option is "ignore the data": all b = 0.
    
      
    For stocks, it always said no
    "Ignore the data" won for SPY in 46 of 46 months. The surprises never had a track record of predicting next month's stock return, so the forecast was simply the historical average, 0.75% a month.
    For bonds it did use them, in 30 of 46 months.
    
      
    Why that is not a failure
    Markets react to a surprise within minutes of 8:30 am. By month-end the news is in the price. A model that refuses to overfit stale news is behaving correctly, and it makes the deterministic model an honest floor.
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    05
    Approach two: an investment committee of AIs.
    Three GPT-4o roles read the masked numbers. Two argue independently; one decides. Everything they said was recorded once and is replayed here word for word.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    The economist
    Asked for a macro view and a next-month return for each fund. It cannot see what anyone else says.
    
      
    The risk manager
    Same numbers, different job: what could go wrong. It runs at the same time as the economist, so neither can anchor on the other.
    
      
    The CIO decides
    Reads both views, says where it sided with whom and why, and sets the numbers: next-month SPY and TLT returns and a confidence word.
    Temperature 0 and a fixed seed, so the same month gives the same answer and every decision can be audited.
    
      
    From words to a view
    The CIO's numbers are not used raw. Like every approach, they are read the way they have worked on past months.
    With the strict rule (direction kept), the committee's SPY call had no track record of being right (k = 0 in all 46 months), but its bond call did, in all 46.
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    06
    Approach three: a model that answers in numbers.
    Jev, from TypeSafe, is a decision model. Instead of prose it returns calibrated probabilities to typed questions. It reads the same masked block.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Question one: a yes/no, as a probability
    "Over the next month, will SPY beat TLT?" Jev answered 37%: leaning no.
    
      
    Question two: pick a tilt
    Five options from strong equities to strong bonds. Jev put 57% on lean bonds, with a confidence of 0.46.
    expected tilt = Σ P(option) × points = −1.23
    
      
    Read in reverse
    In 2015–2019, Jev's bearish months were followed by good stock months. A regression free to choose any sign learned to flip Jev: its "lean bonds" became a bullish 1.41% stock view.
    The flip did not hold in 2019–2022, and cost money.
    
      
    With the direction kept
    Forbid the flip and Jev's view has nothing left: k = 0 in nearly every month, and Jev becomes the placebo.
    Across 139 months it leaned stocks 48 times, bonds 50 and stayed neutral 41, never with more than 0.51 confidence.
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    07
    Approach four: make the AI show its reasoning.
    A plain answer can be insight or a hunch. Causal RAG forces the AI to name a chain of cause and effect, then tests every link against evidence from before the decision.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    A map of what economists claim
    CauseTrace mined 347,571 cause → effect claims from economics papers. Each claim is an edge: "rate hikes → lower equity valuations".
    Edges carry no sign: papers that disagree land on the same edge, and the agents must argue it out.
    
      
    One map per year
    The graph is rebuilt for each decision year from papers dated before it, so 2015 decisions use only what was published by 2014. Twelve vintages, 2015 to 2026.
    
      
    The Hypothesis Agent
    Given this month's trigger, a CPI surprise or an FOMC statement, it picks a path through the graph and states a signed prediction: "hot CPI → 2-year yield up → stocks down this month."
    
      
    The Testing Agent
    It checks each link against verbatim quotes and against the data: did the 2-year yield actually rise? It also tests the opposite story. Verdict, by majority of three runs: Supported, Contradicted or Insufficient.
    
      
    Where it stands
    To keep the cost at zero, it has so far run on a small free open model, qwen3:4b, as a stand-in. OpenAI's models are the planned choice. On that stand-in it has completed all 95 development months (2,726 model calls).
    It has not yet passed its reliability gates: 57% accuracy on labelled cases against 80% required, and 97.7% well-formed answers against 98%. So its results are exploratory and are not counted in the verdict.
    When it passes, its verdicts plug into the same machine as everyone else's.
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

    



    
  
    08
    A fair fight means everything else is identical.
    The first comparisons were not fair: different funds, different starting mixes, a 12-month question scored over one month. We levelled each one.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Same funds, same mix
    Every approach allocates between SPY and TLT around 60/40, bounded at 40–80% stocks. Earlier, the deterministic model held a different mix, and most of Jev's apparent lead came from simply holding more stocks.
    
      
    Same question
    The committee and Jev were originally asked about the next 12 months but scored on the next one. We asked both again, for every month since 2015, about the next month only.
    
      
    Same translation, same trust
    One rule turns any signal into a view, learned only on past months. One rule sets trust, Ω, from each approach's own past misses. No approach sets its own confidence.
    
      
    Same scorecard, plus a placebo
    Same trading days, prices, cash rate and 5 bp cost. And a placebo: the whole machine run on the historical average. If an approach cannot beat the placebo, its signal added nothing.
    
    
    
    
    Held identical for every approach

      10
      Nobody beat doing nothing.
      Every approach, under either translation rule and at every starting mix we tried, scored below static 60/40.
      
  
      
    
      
      
      Read the chart
      Each dot is an approach's score minus static 60/40, in points a year. The line is its 95% range. Left of zero is worse than doing nothing.
      
      
      The machine alone costs 0.4 points
      The placebo, which only tilts on historical averages, loses about 0.4 points a year. It trades no more than static 60/40, so the loss is the tilting itself: small moves away from 60/40 that carry no information. That is the hurdle before any signal can show value. The deterministic model lands on top of it.
      
      
      The committee came closest
      About −0.2 points: it beat the placebo, mainly through its bond calls. But its range runs from about −1.1 to +0.7, so it cannot be told apart from zero.
      
      
      Jev had nothing to add
      With its direction kept, Jev is the placebo. Allowed to be read in reverse, it lost a further 0.7 points.

      11
      Why nothing won.
      
  
      
    
      
      
      The inputs were already in the price
      Every approach saw numbers the market priced weeks earlier: returns, volatility, rate ranks, surprises traded within minutes. There was little left to forecast at a one-month horizon. The placebo matching the deterministic model shows it.
      
      
      Every tilt costs money
      Tilting on a view with no information loses on average, even before costs: the placebo shows it. Tilting harder costs more: the committee traded about 0.5 times its portfolio a year against 0.3 for static 60/40.
      
      
      One month is short
      Macro and valuation effects, where they exist, play out over quarters and years. Next month's stock-versus-bond return is close to a coin toss.
      
      
      Four years cannot see a small edge
      The error bars are 1 to 3 points wide. A real 0.5-point edge would be invisible. "Nobody won" means "nobody won by enough to show in 46 months".

      13
      What would change the answer.
      
  
        
    
      1. Give the AI something the price does not know yet
        Text dated inside the month: FOMC statements, earnings calls, the Causal RAG document corpus. This is how quant firms use language models today: to read, not to forecast.
        2. 
    
      2. Let it say "no view"
        A no-trade band, so months without a strong view keep exactly 60/40. The placebo's 0.4-point loss comes entirely from small tilts that carry no information.
        3. 
    
      3. Use its disagreement as uncertainty
        Ask the committee five times; when its answers scatter, trust it less. Farnoosh Memari's empirical-uncertainty method does exactly this.
        4. 
    
      4. More months
        The AI answers already go back to 2004. A longer Bloomberg export of the surprises would let every approach run 2004–2022: about 220 months instead of 46.
        5. 
    
      5. Open the sealed window once
        2023–2026 has never been scored. After the team signs off the design, it is opened one time, and that result is the one that counts.
