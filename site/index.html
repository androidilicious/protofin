can an AI committee beat a 60/40 portfolio? a controlled test
Dashboard Backtest Jev Uncertainty The story Level field

Every month, decide how much to hold in stocks and how much in bonds.
Let the maths decide how much to trust the forecast.

Protofin is a Duke capstone with BNY. Three forecasting approaches, one a classic quant model and two built on AI, each give a monthly view on US stocks (SPY) and long Treasuries (TLT). The same allocation engine turns each view into a portfolio, and the same backtest scores it against simply holding 60/40. Every page on this site replays answers that were recorded once; nothing here calls a model.

3 · The formulas, in plain words
Surprise (deterministic model)
z = (actual − consensus) ÷ std of the last 12 surprises

How unusual this release was. Markets price what they expected, so only the surprise is news.

View Q (every approach)
Q = average past return + k × (signal − its mean), k ≥ 0 learned on past months

The signal is read the way it has worked before. A signal that never helped gets k = 0 and the view is just the average. The same rule applies to all approaches.

Uncertainty Ω
Ω = average squared miss of that approach's past forecasts

The number that sets trust. A forecast that has typically missed by 6% a month gets little say. No approach sets its own trust.

Baseline π (implied equilibrium)
π = δ × Σ × w   (δ = 2.5, w = 60/40, Σ = covariance of the last 36 months)

The returns that make 60/40 the best mix. Not a forecast; the starting point.

Blend (Black-Litterman)
μ = π + [τΣ ÷ (τΣ + Ω)] × (Q − π)   (τ = 0.05)

Move from the baseline toward the view, by as much as the view is trusted. In practice the view gets 1–5% of the say.

Weights
maximise wᵀμ − (δ/2) wᵀΣw, with Σw = 1 and 40% ≤ wSPY ≤ 80%

More weight to the fund with more expected return per unit of risk. Feed in π alone and you get back about 60/40.

Score (net CER)
net CER = 12 × (mean monthly return above cash − 1.25 × its variance), after 5 bps per dollar traded

A yearly return penalised for bumpiness. Each approach is compared with holding its own 60/40 mix, with a bootstrap for the error bars. The bar to clear is not "make money"; it is "make more than doing nothing".

4 · What keeps the test honest
Masked inputs

The AI sees only relative numbers: returns, ranks, basis-point changes. No dates, names or price levels, so a model that has read history cannot recognise the month. A year-guess probe checks the mask holds.

Point in time

Every input is dated; nothing after the month-end decision is used. Training labels lag two months so an unfinished month never leaks. Prices are hash-pinned and dividend-checked against the fund issuers.

A sealed test window

2019–2022 is for development. 2023–2026 is locked; the evaluator refuses to score it until the team signs a GO record, so nothing can be tuned to the answer.

5 · The result so far

No approach beats holding 60/40 on the 46 development months, at any prior mix, under either view rule. The committee comes closest (about −0.3 to −0.4 points a year), Jev's view carries no usable direction, and the deterministic model behaves like the placebo. The error bars are ±1 to ±3 points, so a small true edge would be invisible on four years.

This matches the careful papers in the field: small or no edge, wide error bars. The honest reading is that with price-derived inputs at a monthly horizon, 60/40 is close to the best anyone can do. To beat it, the AI needs inputs the market has not priced yet.
