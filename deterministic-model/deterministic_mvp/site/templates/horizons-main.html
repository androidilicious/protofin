How the extension works

Targets, overlap and trading are recorded separately.

A longer target is a new experiment. It cannot be inferred from a one-month model or from a rolling evaluation window.

Target completion

A three- or six-month return enters training only after its full holding interval has finished. The same rule applies inside each chronological tuning fold.

Forecast sample

Every eligible monthly decision receives a longer target, even when neighboring targets overlap. MAE, RMSE and OOS R² describe those predictions with that dependence intact.

Portfolio schedule

Portfolio positions hold for the full target horizon. The next position starts only after the prior one exits, so the portfolio sample has fewer observations than the forecast sample.

A rolling 12-month evaluation window is not a 12-month forecast.

It compounds a sequence of shorter holding-period decisions. The new horizon targets predict three or six months directly, with their own completed-target timing.

Interpretation

What this extension can—and cannot—show.

Forecast skill comes first

Compare each asset with the expanding historical mean on the same eligible monthly dates. A positive portfolio return does not prove that the longer forecast is more accurate.

Six months has few trades

The six-month portfolio has only a small number of non-overlapping holdings through 2022. Treat its portfolio return and drawdown as descriptive.

Rules stay fixed

Input safeguards, policy eligibility checks, Ridge tuning and allocation limits inherit the corrected primary. Horizon-specific results are not selected to recover an earlier return.

Reserved years remain reserved

The extension ends with the December 2022 development holding month. No 2023-onward target or portfolio outcome is evaluated.
