Part 1 · How the model works

How does economic news become a portfolio?

First understand the investment process. Then see how our framework tests changes to it.

  1. 01 · Economic surprises

    What differed from expectations?

    Subtract the consensus forecast from each actual release. Divide by the spread of earlier forecast misses so different releases can be compared.

    Surprise = (actual − consensus) ÷ earlier miss standard deviation
  2. 02 · Model inputs

    Summarize the economic picture.

    Average the available standardized releases into Growth and Inflation signals. Add financial conditions, geopolitical risk and eligible policy information; the reference model also includes specified interactions.

    A positive surprise means above consensus; it does not automatically mean “buy stocks.”
  3. 03 · Return forecasts

    Learn from earlier months.

    The reference model uses a separate Ridge regression for each asset’s next-month return. Ridge limits coefficient size. Training-only input limits and policy-history checks address unstable inputs.

    The method experiment compares Ridge with simpler forecasts using its own shared input set.
  4. 04 · Black–Litterman

    How much should we trust the forecast?

    Combine the model’s predictions with reference returns implied by the 45 / 35 / 20 mix and historical asset risk. Earlier completed forecast errors determine view uncertainty; greater uncertainty reduces a forecast’s influence.

    This produces adjusted return estimates. Portfolio weights are chosen in the next step.
  5. 05 · Portfolio allocation

    Turn estimates into holdings.

    Choose weights using adjusted returns, how the assets move together, risk and a penalty for changing holdings. Apply allocation limits, then account for trading costs.

    SPY: U.S. stocks · IEF: U.S. Treasuries · LQD: investment-grade corporate bonds

A small surprise example

If the actual release is 100, consensus is 90 and the earlier miss standard deviation is 5, the standardized surprise is +2: (100 − 90) ÷ 5.

Illustrative numbers, not an observed release. This measures surprise relative to expectations, not how good the economy is.

What is our contribution?

The underlying investment process is the starting point. Our contribution is a framework for testing added signals, forecasting methods and forecast horizons under controlled conditions.

Inputs, learners and target horizons can change within an experiment. The portfolio construction rules stay shared within that comparison.

What do risk, uncertainty and allocation limits mean here?

Risk and uncertainty are different.

Asset risk (Σ) describes how SPY, IEF and LQD moved together over the earlier 36 months, with at least 24 required. Forecast uncertainty (Ω) uses the variance of each asset’s earlier completed prediction errors, with at least 12 required.

The reference returns are implied by the chosen benchmark mix and historical risk. They are not a separate economic forecast.

A stronger forecast need not mean a larger weight.

The allocation step considers all three assets together. The corrected reference allows SPY 20–70%, IEF 15–60% and LQD 10–40%, with weights adding to 100%, on a one-percentage-point grid.

Holdings drift between trades. Costs are 5 basis points per dollar traded, using the full absolute change in weights. The 45 / 35 / 20 mix is the initially funded reference, not a reset every month.

1 · Information at the decision

The starting inputs

These are input values before the model’s training-window processing. Growth and Inflation average standardized release surprises. Index values and surprise scores use different units.

Economic histories include later revisions and assumed availability dates. This is not a complete reconstruction of what was known in real time.

2 · Learn from earlier outcomes

What could training use?

Missing values, input limits, scaling and Ridge tuning use earlier training data. Unsupported policy features contribute zero.

The inputs alone do not explain why an individual forecast moved; that requires examining the fitted coefficients and processed features.
