Our backtesting and evaluation framework

Follow the evidence,
from inputs to outcomes.

The framework connects what was known at a decision to a forecast, a portfolio and a realized result. It helps us test a change without confusing its effect with changes in timing, trading rules or the comparison sample.

One connected process

Five steps, with a record of each decision.

The saved outputs let us inspect where a result came from rather than looking only at the final return.

  1. 01

    Set the information cutoff

    Build macro surprises and risk inputs using the observations treated as available by the decision. Keep source dates and availability assumptions.

  2. 02

    Train and predict

    Learn from earlier completed returns. Fit missing-value treatment, input limits, scaling and tuning within each training window.

  3. 03

    Account for uncertainty

    Black–Litterman combines forecasts with a historical-risk reference. Each model’s uncertainty comes from its own earlier completed forecast errors.

  4. 04

    Build and trade the portfolio

    Choose SPY, IEF and LQD weights within fixed limits. Trade after the decision, account for drift and charge the same cost rule.

  5. 05

    Evaluate what happened

    Compare prediction errors, returns, daily losses, turnover and costs. Check the full sample before focusing on individual episodes.

What changes—and what stays fixed?

“Same rules” applies within an experiment. The two completed studies have different input sets and slightly different coverage.

No unfinished training outcomes

A return target must finish before it can enter training or an earlier-error estimate. The same timing check applies inside tuning folds.

Compare the same dates

Use matched observations within each study. A method with a shorter usable history cannot quietly receive an easier comparison period.

Carry the portfolio forward

Holdings drift with asset returns and are not reset at episode boundaries. Costs use the full weight change from those drifted holdings.

Two levels of evaluation

A good return is only part of the evidence.

Simple benchmarks make the added value of macro information easier to assess.

First: were the predictions useful?

Compare each asset with a forecast of its past average return.

  • Average miss (MAE): how far off were forecasts?
  • RMSE: did large mistakes dominate?
  • OOS R²: did squared errors beat the past-average forecast?
  • Extreme predictions: were any forecasts implausibly large?

Then: did the portfolio improve?

Use the same construction and cost rules within the study.

  • Total return after trading costs
  • Largest daily fall from a previous peak
  • Trading turnover, costs and weight-limit frequency
  • Performance across years, episodes and rolling windows

Rolling windows overlap, and small episodes are not independent experiments. Bootstrap intervals are exploratory and conditional on the saved portfolio paths; they do not refit models or correct for trying multiple variants.

The original model’s +589% SPY prediction exposed a sparse policy input with a tiny training scale. We traced it, compared repairs, and reran a corrected reference model. This is a quality check within the research—not its whole contribution.

The correction uses earlier-data input limits and explicit policy-history and variation checks. The original 23.99% remains a diagnostic comparison. The initial added-signal runs have not yet been repeated with this corrected specification.

Limits and the final test

State what the evidence cannot yet establish.

The development work supports comparisons and diagnostics. It is not an untouched confirmation of a selected strategy.

Revised histories

Some economic values were revised later. Availability lags approximate historical access; they do not reconstruct all real-time vintages.

Short evaluation history

There are few independent stress episodes. Year and boundary checks help describe sensitivity but do not establish broad robustness.

Different study scopes

The original signal study, compact model comparison and corrected reference answer different questions. Their results should not be pooled into one ranking.

Reserved years not evaluated here

The team must confirm whether 2023 onward remains unexamined before a separately authorized final test. Longer forecast horizons also remain untested.
