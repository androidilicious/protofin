# Literature behind the results

Our level-playing-field result (no approach beats holding the fixed mix) rests on three lessons. Each matches long-standing findings.

## 1. Forecasts built on already-priced data behave like noise

- Welch, I. and Goyal, A. (2008). A comprehensive look at the empirical performance of equity premium prediction. *Review of Financial Studies* 21(4). Dozens of classic predictors almost never beat the historical average out of sample (our placebo result).
- Campbell, J. Y. and Thompson, S. B. (2008). Predicting excess stock returns out of sample: can anything beat the historical average? *Review of Financial Studies* 21(4). Predictability is small and helps only with restrictions on the forecasts.
- Lopez-Lira, A. and Tang, Y. (2023). Can ChatGPT forecast stock price movements? Return predictability and large language models. Working paper. The edge came from reading fresh news headlines at a one-day horizon, not from already-public numbers.
- Li, W. W., Kim, H., Cucuringu, M. and Ma, T. (2026). Can LLM-based financial investing strategies outperform the market in long run? *KDD '26*. arXiv:2505.07078. Short-window LLM gains vanish over 2004–2024.

## 2. Acting on noisy forecasts costs money

- Michaud, R. O. (1989). The Markowitz optimization enigma: is "optimized" optimal? *Financial Analysts Journal* 45(1). Optimizers maximise estimation error.
- DeMiguel, V., Garlappi, L. and Uppal, R. (2009). Optimal versus naive diversification: how inefficient is the 1/N portfolio strategy? *Review of Financial Studies* 22(5). Equal weights beat 14 optimized strategies out of sample.
- Black, F. and Litterman, R. (1992). Global portfolio optimization. *Financial Analysts Journal* 48(5). Anchoring to a neutral mix limits the damage from noisy views; the role Black-Litterman plays in our pipeline.

## 3. Short or repeated tests mislead

- Bailey, D. H., Borwein, J. M., López de Prado, M. and Zhu, Q. J. (2014). Pseudo-mathematics and financial charlatanism: the effects of backtest overfitting on out-of-sample performance. *Notices of the AMS* 61(5).
- Harvey, C. R., Liu, Y. and Zhu, H. (2016). ... and the cross-section of expected returns. *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1).
- Glasserman, P. and Lin, C. (2023). Assessing look-ahead bias in stock return predictions generated by GPT sentiment analysis. Working paper.
- Sarkar, S. K. and Vafa, K. (2024). Lookahead bias in pretrained language models. Working paper.
